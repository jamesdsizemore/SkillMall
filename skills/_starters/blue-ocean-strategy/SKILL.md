# Blue Ocean Strategy

Apply the Blue Ocean Strategy framework to identify uncontested market space rather than competing in overcrowded markets. Uses the Four Actions Framework (ERRC) and Strategy Canvas to create differentiation that makes competition irrelevant.

## When to use

- Stuck competing on the same dimensions as every competitor
- Looking for new markets or user segments
- Evaluating whether to enter a crowded market
- Redesigning a value proposition to stand out

## Four Actions Framework (ERRC Grid)

The ERRC grid forces explicit choices about what you will and will not do.

**Eliminate** — What factors does the industry compete on that create no value for buyers? Cut them entirely. These are costs without customer benefit.

**Reduce** — What factors should be reduced well below industry standard? Compete less hard on things that matter less than the industry assumes.

**Raise** — What factors should be raised well above industry standard? Compete harder on things that actually drive buyer satisfaction.

**Create** — What factors should be created that the industry has never offered? This is where genuine differentiation comes from.

## Strategy Canvas

The strategy canvas plots competing factors on the X axis and offering level on the Y axis. Plot one curve per competitor and one for your offering.

Seek curves that are genuinely different from competitors — not just higher on every dimension.

## Non-customer analysis

Three tiers of non-customers:

1. **Soon-to-be:** Use your market but reluctantly. Identify why they leave.
2. **Refusing:** Never considered your market. What turned them away?
3. **Unexplored:** Never been targeted. What would make the market relevant to them?

## What Blue Ocean is NOT

Not about beating competitors — it is about making them irrelevant by creating new demand. If your strategy is still defined by competitors, it is not a blue ocean.
