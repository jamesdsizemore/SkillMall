# Sprint Planning

Run effective sprint planning meetings that produce a committed sprint backlog with a clear goal, realistic capacity, and broken-down tasks. Based on the Scrum Guide.

## When to use

- Before every sprint to plan the next sprint's work
- When the team is consistently over- or under-committing
- When sprint goals are vague or missing
- When sprint planning meetings run over 2 hours

## Sprint length

Your team's sprint length: [FILL-IN: sprint-length]

## Sprint planning: two parts

**Part 1 — WHAT (30-60 min):** The Product Owner presents the top backlog items. The team and PO collaborate on a Sprint Goal. The team selects stories from the backlog that will achieve the goal.

**Part 2 — HOW (30-60 min):** The team breaks selected stories into tasks. Each task ≤ 1 day, assigned to one person. The team commits to the sprint backlog.

## Sprint goal

The sprint goal answers: "Why are we doing this sprint?" It is a single sentence that provides coherence — the team can re-prioritize tasks within the sprint without changing the goal.

Good: "Users can create and deploy a skill without CLI setup by end of sprint."
Bad: "Complete tickets 101, 102, 103, 107."

## Capacity planning

Your team velocity: [FILL-IN: team-velocity]

Capacity formula:
- Available developer-days = (team size) × (sprint days) - PTO - recurring meetings
- Convert to story points using recent velocity
- Load sprint to 80% of capacity — reserve 20% for surprises

## Definition of Done

A story is Done only when: [FILL-IN: definition-of-done]

Apply DoD to every story during planning. Stories that cannot meet DoD within the sprint should not be committed.

## Warning signs

- Sprint backlog has no Sprint Goal → no coherence
- Stories larger than half a sprint → break them down or defer
- Team committed to 100% capacity → no slack for surprises
- No task breakdown → hidden unknowns
