# Content Strategy Planning

Build a content strategy from first principles: audience first, goal second, content types third, distribution fourth, measurement last. This is the correct order. Most teams do it backwards — they start with "let's write a blog post" and work backward to justify it.

## When to Use

- Building a new content program from scratch
- Auditing and restructuring an existing content operation
- Aligning a content calendar to a specific business goal
- Creating briefs for individual content pieces within a strategy
- Do NOT use when: the business goal is undefined — content strategy cannot substitute for product-market clarity

## Step 1 — Define the Audience

A valid audience definition answers: who are they, what role do they hold, what company type and size, what are they trying to accomplish, and what are they skeptical about? Vague personas ("developers," "marketers") produce vague content.

Write the persona as a sentence: "Senior software engineers at 50-500 person SaaS companies who are evaluating observability tools and are skeptical of vendor lock-in."

## Step 2 — Define the Primary Goal

**[FILL-IN: content-goal]** — Replace with the specific business goal content must serve.

Goals must be measurable. "Build brand awareness" is not a goal. "Generate 200 inbound demo requests per month from organic search" is a goal. Every piece of content in the strategy should trace back to this goal. If a piece cannot trace back to it, cut it or defer it.

## Step 3 — Content Audit

Before creating new content, audit what exists. Score each piece on:
- **Traffic** — monthly organic sessions
- **Conversion** — does it generate leads, signups, or demo requests?
- **Freshness** — is it accurate? When was it last updated?
- **Funnel stage** — awareness, consideration, or decision?

Categorize each piece: Keep as-is, Update, Consolidate (merge with another piece), or Delete. Most teams have 40-60% of content in the "delete or consolidate" bucket. Pruning improves domain authority.

## Step 4 — Content Types by Funnel Stage

Assign content types to funnel stages deliberately:

**Awareness (top of funnel)** — the audience doesn't know you yet
- Long-form SEO content targeting high-volume keywords
- Thought leadership on problems the audience searches for
- Comparison content ("X vs. Y") where your audience is evaluating options

**Consideration (middle of funnel)** — the audience is evaluating solutions
- Case studies with measurable customer outcomes
- Technical deep dives that demonstrate product capability
- Webinars and live Q&A with practitioners

**Decision (bottom of funnel)** — the audience is ready to buy or try
- ROI calculators and business case templates
- Free trial or demo-request-focused landing pages
- Migration guides ("switching from X to your tool")

## Step 5 — Editorial Calendar

Plan 8 weeks ahead minimum. Planning 2 weeks ahead means you are always in reactive mode.

## Step 6 — SEO Integration

Every content piece must have one target keyword assigned at the brief stage, before writing begins. The keyword research workflow:

1. Identify 5-10 topics relevant to your audience's problems
2. Use a keyword tool (Ahrefs, Semrush, Google Search Console) to find high-volume, low-competition variants
3. Map one keyword per piece — never compete two pieces on the same keyword
4. Interlink related pieces after publication to build topical authority

## Step 7 — Distribution Plan

Publishing is 40% of the work. For each piece, define before publication where it goes:

- Email newsletter (segment: all subscribers, or specific segment)
- LinkedIn (organic post, employee amplification, or paid)
- Relevant online communities (Slack groups, Reddit, Discord — where your audience actually is)
- Repurposed formats: thread, short video, slides deck

## Step 8 — Measuring Content

Report on metrics that connect to the business goal, not just page views:

| Metric | What it measures | Report cadence |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| Organic sessions | Discovery | Monthly |
| Email signups from content | Audience building | Monthly |
| Demo requests from content | Goal conversion | Weekly |
| Content-influenced pipeline | Revenue impact | Quarterly |

Page views are a vanity metric unless your goal is advertising revenue.
