# Stakeholder Communication

Write status updates and project communications that give stakeholders exactly what they need — current state, risks, and decisions — without burying the headline in background.

## When to use

- Weekly project status report to leadership or sponsors
- Escalating a blocker that needs a decision
- Communicating a timeline change or scope reduction
- Preparing an executive summary for a milestone review

## Bottom-line-up-front

Executives read the first paragraph and nothing else unless there is a problem. Put the key fact — status, change, decision needed — in the first sentence. Details go below.

Wrong: "Over the past two weeks the team has been working on the authentication system and we have made some good progress although there are a few things we need to discuss..."

Right: "Authentication is GREEN. Login, logout, and session refresh are complete. One decision needed by Thursday: whether to support magic links (see below)."

## RAG status

- **RED:** Significant risk to scope, timeline, or budget. Needs immediate attention or decision.
- **AMBER:** Risk identified. Mitigation in progress. Watch for escalation.
- **GREEN:** On track. No decisions needed.

Put the RAG status in the subject line: "[GREEN] Auth service — Week of May 18".

## What to include

- RAG status and one-sentence summary
- 3-5 accomplishments from the past week (outcomes, not activities)
- 2-3 items planned for next week
- Blockers: what is blocked, who needs to unblock it, by when
- Decisions needed: specific question, options, recommendation, deadline

## What to exclude

- Implementation details (save for the technical doc linked below)
- Completed task lists (unless someone asked for them)
- Percentage complete on any task (meaningless without context)
- Jargon your audience does not share
